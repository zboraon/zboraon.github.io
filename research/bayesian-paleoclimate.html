Bayesian methods for palaeoclimate reconstruction

Bayesian modelling is useful when palaeoclimate evidence is incomplete, heterogeneous or uncertain. Rather than producing only a single reconstructed curve, Bayesian approaches can represent uncertainty in observations, latent climate states, regression parameters and transition timing.

Regional synthesis

The eastern Mediterranean temperature study combines many site-level reconstructions with multilevel regression so that shared regional structure and between-record variability are represented together.

Missing-data reconstruction

The Lake Ohrid study combines latent state-space models with SSVS to infer missing stable-isotope values from coeval proxy information while propagating uncertainty.

Testing abrupt events

The 4.2 ka studies use Bayesian structural time series and change-point models to test whether a proposed event is actually supported by individual palaeoclimate records.
