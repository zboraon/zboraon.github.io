Research article · 2025

A conceptual and statistical framework for delineating the timing of a stratigraphic transition: Holocene-Anthropocene boundary as a case study

Zeki Bora Ön, Mustafa Efe Ateş, Jérôme Kaiser · Progress in Physical Geography: Earth and Environment 49(1–2), 63–83 (2025)

Publisher / DOICode / data

What this paper does

This study asks how a chronostratigraphic transition should be dated when evidence comes from multiple sites and proxies with measurement and age uncertainty. It combines multilevel Bayesian change-point analysis with an errors-in-variables treatment and applies the framework to plutonium isotopes, spheroidal carbonaceous particles and radiocarbon records.

Main finding

The population-level change point for plutonium is estimated at 1953 CE, with a 1951.5–1954.5 interval. The analysis also shows regional structure in SCP signals and a lag in radiocarbon response, illustrating why a transition inferred from multiple records can differ from a date selected at a single candidate stratotype.

When this paper may be useful

  • Dating stratigraphic or environmental transitions from multiple records
  • Hierarchical Bayesian change-point models
  • Age and measurement uncertainty
  • Errors-in-variables problems
  • Testing whether regional records share a common transition
