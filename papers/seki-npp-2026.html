Non-pollen palynomorphs (NPPs) and their role in reconstructing the paleoclimate of the Seki-Eceler Höyük during the Holocene in southwestern Türkiye

Zahra Hashemi, Demet Biltekin, Alan M. Greaves, Sena Akçer-Ön, Zeki Bora Ön, Memet Namık Çağatay, Ömer Yetemen · Quaternary International 785, 110488 (2026)

Read PDF Publisher / DOI Download BibTeX catalogue

What this study does

This paper analyses non-pollen palynomorphs (NPPs)—including freshwater algae, fungal spores and other microscopic remains—from the 4.7 m JARK14-04 sediment core next to Seki-Eceler Höyük in southwestern Türkiye. The record spans approximately the last 12,000 years and complements existing pollen and archaeological evidence to investigate changes in wetland habitats, freshwater availability, catchment disturbance and potential human activity.

Main findings

Freshwater-related NPPs such as Azolla, Spirogyra and Zygnema indicate relatively extensive aquatic and wetland environments during the early Holocene. Changes between approximately 8200 and 7700 years BP are consistent with reduced freshwater availability and greater catchment disturbance, potentially related to the 8.2 ka climatic event; local hydrological effects cannot be excluded.

In the middle Holocene, Sporormiella-type spores suggest herbivore presence and possible grazing, while Thecaphora and Tilletia may support interpretations of disturbed vegetation or agricultural activity when considered alongside independent evidence. From around 3500 years BP onward, the assemblage is consistent with increased catchment disturbance resulting from interacting hydroclimatic variability and human pressure.

Chronological and analytical approach

The study uses NPP assemblage classification and a revised Bayesian age-depth model constructed with rbacon in R. The chronology incorporates ten radiocarbon measurements, the IntCal20 calibration curve and a core-top age constraint. Potentially anomalous radiocarbon determinations are handled with Student’s t likelihoods; chronological uncertainty remains conditional on the chosen likelihoods and priors.

The paper also documents numerous EIES-coded morphotypes. Taxonomically unresolved forms are retained as a morphological reference and are not treated as independent environmental indicators.

When this paper may be useful

  • Holocene palaeoenvironmental reconstruction in southwestern Anatolia and the eastern Mediterranean
  • Interpreting non-pollen palynomorphs, freshwater algae and fungal spores as environmental proxies
  • Investigating the possible local expression of the 8.2 ka climate event
  • Studying interactions between hydrological change, catchment erosion, grazing and land use
  • Combining multiproxy palaeoenvironmental evidence with Bayesian radiocarbon age-depth modelling
  • Distinguishing confidently identified NPP taxa from unresolved morphological types
